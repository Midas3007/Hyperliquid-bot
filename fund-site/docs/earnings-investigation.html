Why earnings dropped, and how to fix it

Source: every token transfer of the bot wallet (21,690 transfers, 8,700 transactions, 4,320 trades) and every swap in the two Uniswap pools it trades (24,659 swaps), read from the Celo chain for 29 Oct – 28 Nov. Totals match the fund's own trade history. Profits are before gas; Lagos time.
TL;DR
  • Earnings fell in four steps: $24.61 a day (29 Oct–11 Nov) → $4.93 (12–19 Nov) → $5.32 (20–22 Nov) → −$0.29 a day since careful mode (23–28 Nov).
  • The main cause is competition, not a lack of opportunity. Uniswap volume in the wARS pool more than doubled (≈$83k → $204k a day), but our share fell from 40% to 3%. New arbitrage contracts arrived on 13 Nov, 19 Nov and 24 Nov. They do both legs in one transaction and take the gaps before we can.
  • Our Uniswap leg leaks. On Textile-first trades, the gap at Textile shrank from 5.0 to 2.6 bps, while the loss between the two legs grew from 2.4 to 4.2 bps. Bots move the pool in the 2–3 seconds between our legs.
  • Careful mode made it worse. Its Textile re-check blocks our best route (Uniswap-first: 96% profitable, +6.7 bps) and lets the worst one through (Textile-first: 34% profitable, −1.0 bps). Peak-hour trades of $450–600 won only 12% of the time.
  • Dips added risk: the peso fell 7% (1,650 → 1,765 ARS/$) and the naira 6% (1,372 → 1,455 NGN/$) in mid-November, with 3–8% intraday swings. On 14, 17 and 19 Nov, liquidity was pulled from the wARS pool.
  • The fix: flip careful mode today (let Uniswap-first through; require ~5 bps for Textile-first; no peak upsizing). Next, protect the Uniswap leg and add liquidity and volatility guards. The structural fix is an atomic arbitrage contract, which is how the competitors trade.

Our two legs are 2–3 seconds apart. In phase A almost nothing happened in that window. Now a bot often trades the pool first, so the price we sell at is worse than the price we planned on. A Textile-first trade now needs a gap of more than ~4–5 bps to survive. The typical gap is 2.6 bps.

The Textile re-check before a Uniswap-first trade cut those trades by ~80%. They are the best trades we have, and they were almost always profitable even before careful mode (95% in phase C). The unwinds the re-check was meant to stop cost $1.87 in phase C, against $11.34 made on the trades it now blocks. Meanwhile Textile-first trades, which careful mode does not check against Uniswap, became the bulk of the activity and lose money. Peak hours still allow bigger trades: in phase D the 48 trades of $450–600 were only 12% profitable (−$10.10).

5 · Cause 4: dips, devaluation and pulled liquidity

Uniswap pool rate vs 29 Oct (up = local currency weaker):ARS per USD (wARS)NGN per USD (cNGN)
  • Peso: 1,650 → 1,765 ARS/$ (+7%), mostly 13–21 Nov. Intraday ranges hit 7.6% on 18 Nov (a spike to 1,810) and 3–4% on 23–24 Nov, when the one-way seller 0xdc7d… arrived.
  • Naira: 1,372 → 1,455 NGN/$ (+6%), 10–19 Nov, with 2–3% intraday swings on 18–19 Nov.
  • Liquidity pulls: liquidity in the wARS pool dropped to about 25% of normal on 14 and 17 Nov, and to about 1% on 19 Nov. We made 2 wARS trades on 19 Nov and none on 20 Nov. Liquidity came back on 21 Nov, about 15% lower than before.

When the currency trends, Textile's quotes and the pool drift apart and back quickly. Bots close those gaps in the same block; we pay for the move when we are halfway through a trade. The −$6.31 day (23 Nov) and the losing days 20, 25 and 27–28 Nov all fall on wide-range or one-way days.

6 · What did not change

  • Textile still offers gaps on the trades we take: 4–6 bps on most days. The opportunity moved to faster players; it did not vanish.
  • Uniswap-first trades are still excellent (96% profitable). The problem is concentrated in Textile-first trades and unwinds.
  • Hold times are unchanged (Textile-first 2–3 s, Uniswap-first 5 s). The bot has not slowed down; the others got faster.

7 · How to fix it (ranked)

1 · Today · settings only
Turn careful mode the right way round. Cut the Textile re-check cushion for Uniswap-first trades from 2 bps to 0.5 bps, so the best route trades again. Require at least 5 bps for Textile-first trades, and re-quote Uniswap at the latest block just before the Textile fill. Cap every trade at $300, peak included, while the bots are active.
Expected: stops the bleeding. Uniswap-first alone made about +$3.80 a day in phase C; Textile-first stops losing about $0.70 a day.
2 · This week · bot code
Protect the Uniswap leg. Set the Uniswap sale's minimum output at the planned price minus 1 bp. If that reverts, keep the coins and sell on Textile, or retry, instead of dumping on Uniswap. Add a liquidity guard (skip a pool whose liquidity is under 50% of its 7-day normal) and a volatility guard (skip when the pool moved more than 0.5% in the last 10 minutes, or when a one-way seller is active).
Expected: removes most of the unwind and dip losses (−$15 of losses in phase D alone).
3 · Two to three weeks · the structural fix
An atomic arbitrage contract. One transaction that fills the Textile order and swaps on Uniswap, and reverts unless the profit is at least the minimum. A losing trade becomes impossible: a failed attempt costs only gas, about $0.0004. This is how the four newcomers trade, and it puts us on equal footing for gaps of 2–5 bps. First check whether Textile's reactor accepts fills from a contract. It needs a careful audit before it holds fund money.
Expected: the only path back towards phase-A share. Even 15–20% of today's pool volume at 2–3 bps is about $8–15 a day.
4 · Ongoing
Go where the bots aren't yet. New corridors on Textile (the VGBP/VCHF check), Mento, and Base (Minakiba: Mel's NGN pools). Keep a weekly version of this check: our share of pool volume is the early warning. When it falls below 20%, act.
5 · Communication
Reset expectations. The first two weeks (5–8% a day on NAV) were an early-market window with almost no competition. Shareholders should hear that returns are now competitive-market returns, and what is being done.
