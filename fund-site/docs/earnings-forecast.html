3. What moves earnings (from our own trades, 15 days, Lagos time)

Margin by hour of day (bps of traded $)
Margin by trade size (bps)
  • Hour: the best margins are 14:00–18:00 (peak 17:00 at 7.3 bps, 16:00 at 6.2) and 21:00–23:00 (21:00 at 5.9). The weakest is 11:00 (0.5 bps, 65% won). Overnight is steady but small.
  • Day: Tuesday earned most (5.9 bps, 82% won); Saturday is weakest (0.9 bps, 69% won). Weekends have fewer trades and thinner gaps.
  • Size: (the under-$100 bar is only 139 trades, so ignore it) trades of $400–600 earned 5.7 bps and made the most money ($115 of $258). Trades over $600 earn 1.8 bps and win only 63%: past that point each trade moves the price against itself. The Uniswap pool holds only about $35k of USDT, so size has a ceiling.
  • Costs: fixed running costs of ~$1.22 a day equal 41% of a Quiet day's profit. On 14 and 18 Nov profit ($0.08, $0.42) did not cover them.
  • Competition and tight rule (not separable yet): margin per traded dollar fell from 4.2 to 1.1 bps week over week while win rate barely moved (76% → 74.5%). That fits calmer gaps and/or other arbitrageurs. The tight first-leg rule has been on throughout, so its effect cannot be isolated.

5. Predictors to watch

  • Naira volatility and Textile volume. Gaps open when the naira moves fast and Uniswap lags Textile's quote. Textile's 24h range and volume are the leading signs. Today's range is 1.9% and the gap is ~0.
  • Hour and weekday. Expect the best flow Mon–Fri 14:00–18:00 and 21:00–23:00 Lagos; weakest on Saturdays and around 11:00.
  • Our size vs the Uniswap pool. Keep trades around $400–600; bigger loses margin. If the pool is topped up, the ceiling rises.
  • Running costs vs profit. Below ~$12 a day of profit the 10% carve does not cover costs, so the pool pays the gap. A fall in margin with no change in win rate is also what new competition looks like.
